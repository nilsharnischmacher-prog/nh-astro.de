Münster (Westphalia), Germany

New Horizons Observatory

My own observatory on the edge of Münster. Most of the large, extended nebulae come from here. With 3 nm narrowband filters it still pulls out fine structure under a light-polluted suburban sky and even with the Moon up.

Site

LocationMünster-Hiltrup, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany
Altitudeapprox. 60 m above sea level
SkyBortle 4, SQM approx. 20.7 mag/arcsec² (World Atlas 2015) – suburban-edge sky
Focuslarge emission nebulae and dark clouds, mostly narrowband (SHO)

Equipment

Main telescopeAstro-Physics 130 GTX (Starfire Gran Turismo) with 0.79× Tri-TCC – f/5, approx. 650 mm
Second telescopeTS Photoline 130 with Riccardi flattener (earlier images, planned for a future dual setup)
CameraZWO ASI6200MM Pro (full frame, cooled, mono)
FiltersChroma L, R, G, B and 3 nm Ha, OIII, SII (50 mm round) in a ZWO EFW 7 × 2″ filter wheel
FocuserMoonlite NiteCrawler WR35 (on both telescopes)
MountSky-Watcher AZ-EQ6 Pro – soon a JTW Trident GTL with Lika 25-bit absolute encoders
DomeScopeDome 3M V3
ControlN.I.N.A.
WeatherAAG CloudWatcher (clouds, sky brightness, rain, wind), all-sky camera

Oukaïmeden, High Atlas (Morocco)

Project Oukaimeden

A remote telescope at the Oukaïmeden Observatory, about 50 km south of Marrakesh. I am a member of the Project Oukaïmeden team. Its sky is among the darkest in North Africa. With 2.8 m of focal length it is ideal for small targets such as planetary nebulae and galaxies.

Site

LocationOukaïmeden Observatory (Cadi Ayyad University), High Atlas, Morocco
Altitudeapprox. 2,700 m
SkyBortle 1 (according to on-site measurements), about 250 clear nights per year
Focussmall targets: planetary nebulae, galaxies, nebula details

Equipment

TelescopeRC14 (Ritchey-Chrétien 356 mm, f/8, 2,821 mm)
Camerafull-frame CMOS, 2×2 binning (0.55″/pixel)
FiltersL, R, G, B, Ha, OIII, SII
